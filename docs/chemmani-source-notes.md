## A. Somadeva & Wanninayake, "Forensic Archaeological Excavation in Chemmani, Jaffna 2025/2026: A summary report on findings"
Interim report. Prologue signed Raj Somadeva PhD, 10 Oct 2026, Colombo. PDF created 15 Oct 2026. Online: sangam.org (uploads/2026/11).
PDF page -> printed page: PDF p4 = printed 1 ... PDF p(n) = printed n-3.
- Prologue (PDF p2): interim; ~80 days of excavation.
- §1 (p4/1): Sittupattu (also spelled Sittupatti) cemetery, Chemmani; J/94 GN division, Nallur DS. "allegedly linked to illicit activities and suspected human rights violations". Remains surfaced accidentally during digging for construction at cemetery. Excavated on Jaffna Magistrate's Court order, observed by Magistrate and Chief JMO Jaffna Hospital.
- §2: phases over ~3 months.
- §3 (p5-6/2): site disturbed by garbage dumping, waste pits, animal carcass burial; "does not exclusively utilize as a human burial ground but, used as a dumping ground for various disposable items including human remains as well." Fig 1: dead cow, hospital waste on surface.
- §4 (p6/3): Trench 1 & 2 chosen from satellite imagery. Trench 1 "[insert number] meters" NW of cremation platform (placeholder left in report). Trench 2 14 m from platform; trenches ~40 m apart.
- §5 (p7/4): Trench 1: 28.5 m x 18 m = 513 m2; depth generally 1.30-1.60 m (max 1.60). Trench 2: trial trenches 0.5 m wide (13.80 m, 6.40 m, 1.40 m), to 0.80 m.
- §6: 3 soil layers; Layer 3 lagoon sand/clay pH>8, most remains in upper/middle of Layer 3.
- §8 (p8/5): "574 minimum number of individual (MNI) human skeletons". Contents page calls §8 "Complete Human Skeletons"; body heading "Identifiable Individual Human Skeletons".
- Fig 3 (p9/6): age categories, N=574, preliminary field evaluations: Adult 30%, Sub-adult 5%, Child 41%, Infant 22% (sums to 98%). Verified from rendered chart.
- §9 (p9/6): 51 accumulations of disarticulated bones; iron nails in some (e.g. AC 51) -> possibly earlier coffin burials disturbed.
- §10 Fig 4 (p10/7): cranial orientation: NW 72%, SE 11%, SW 7%, NE 3%, W 3%, N 1%, E 1%, S 1%, can't id 1%. No uniform pattern (report's wording).
- §11 (p11-12/8-9): hands mostly over abdomen or alongside body; "notable degree of consistency"; Sk 227 & 429 seated; seated posture alone does not establish Samadhi link.
- §12 (p12/9): depths 0.24 m to 1.52 m; many child burials shallow; adults/sub-adults <1.00 m in western sector.
- §13 (p13/10): no discernible horizontal pattern; overlapping -> "successive episodes of interment rather than a single, simultaneous depositional event"; "unlikely to represent a simple, spatially uniform depositional event".
- §14: no preserved textiles; buttons, eyelets, studs found.
- §15 (p14/11): 170 artefact entries; 6 categories. Rings (SK 278), bangles (SK 165), beads mostly with children. Amulets, coins (some with cloth traces). Clothing: plastic buttons, eyelets, plastic studs. SK 25: plastic bag, cut cloth pieces, socks and shoes, metal anklet chain, small nails nearby. 15.5: garlands "pollythen", text says only example is two garlands with a single skeleton but also cites "Sk. 56 and Sk. 48" (internal inconsistency). 15.6 iron nails/fragments.
- Table 1 (p17-18/14): artefact-skeleton associations; caption: "suggests the intentional motive of the burials".
- §16 (p18-19/15-16): coins: SK 367 coin dated 1870 CE (report: minting date does not date the burial). Ash layer to E/SE, 5-6 m from cremation platform; coins dated 1920 (Sk. 172), 1944, 1963, 1975, 1978, 1982, 1991, 1994.
- §17 (p19-20/16): GPR: "numerous subsurface anomalies", "some of which may be consistent with additional burials"; site "may be considerably more extensive than the area investigated".
- §18 (p20/17): "insufficient, in isolation, to support a definitive conclusion"; "greater similarities to contexts characterized by formal burial practices than to clandestine burial sites"; "If the concentration of burials resulted from an epidemic or another form of natural catastrophe..."; "These observations remain provisional."
- Appendix note: sample Sk 1-150, Sk 62, 63, 102, 145 excluded (low-res photos).

## B. Supreme Court, Somaratne Rajapakse & others v Hon. Attorney General (Krishanthi Kumaraswamy rape case)
SC Appeal 2/2002 (TAB); HC Colombo 8778/97. Reported [2010] 2 Sri L.R. 113. Bench: Dr Shirani A. Bandaranayake J (author), Edussuriya J, Yapa J, J.A.N. de Silva J, Jayasinghe J. Argued 12, 17, 27, 28 Nov 2003. Decided 3 Feb 2004.
- p1 (SLR 113): 1st-5th appellants confessed to Military Police; no explanation how they knew independently where bodies and clothing were buried.
- p5 (117): originally 9 accused, 18 counts; all acquitted on counts 1-7 (unlawful assembly).
- p8 (120): 1st appellant a Lance Corporal; on 07.09.1996 assigned to "Chemmuni Security Check Point" (judgment's spelling). 2nd and 4th soldiers at the checkpoint; 3rd a Reserve Constable. Sentences include death sentences on murder counts.
- p9 (121): 5th appellant a Corporal at Forward Defence Line ~50 m away. Victims named in judgment: Krishanthi Kumaraswamy (18, A/L student, Chundikuli College), mother Rasamma Kumaraswamy (59, teacher, former principal), brother Pranavan (16, St John's College), family friend Siddambaram Kirubamoorthi. 7 Sept 1996 she left home by bicycle for her Chemistry exam.
- p11 (123): bodies buried in pits behind checkpoint, clothes in separate pit; anonymous petition -> army inquiry -> four decomposed bodies exhumed close to checkpoint.
- p39 (151): even leaving out the confessions, evidence established guilt beyond reasonable doubt; convictions and sentences affirmed; appeal dismissed; all 4 other judges agreed.

- ASA 37/17/99, 22 June 1999 (News Service 121/99): 2 AI observers + 2 Physicians for Human Rights (USA) forensic experts witnessed, on 16-17 June, local experts exhume 2 bodies from a shallow grave identified by Rajapakse. One blindfolded with a shirt, other hands tied behind back with a vest. Relatives identified them as two garage workers arrested by army 19 Aug 1996. p2: on 16 June Rajapakse said he could identify at least ten places; magistrate ordered him to locate the other nine; excavate before October rains. AI also referenced LTTE burials ~1990.
- ASA 37/30/99, 7 Dec 1999 (News Service 230/99): MoD Board of Investigation reportedly told relatives it cannot establish fate of 374 people arrested in mid-1996 out of more than 700 cases probed. Fate of at least two "not known" although their bodies were exhumed and identified at Chemmani in June. AI experts present at exhumation of remains of 15 people in Sept 1999 and analysis in Oct; "a pattern of injuries had emerged".

## E. HRCSL Fact-Finding Report on Chemmani Mass Grave Site (published 3 Sept 2025) + Press Notice HRC/P/i/E/03/09/25
Mission 3-4 Aug 2025. Team: commissioners Prof. Thaiyamuthu Thanaraj, Prof. Farzana Haniffa, Dr Gehan Gunathilake; Nihal Chandrasiri, K. Kapilan Villavarajan, T. Kanagaraj.
- p2 Background: Feb 2025, construction workers found remains within "Sinthupathy Cemetery" (HRCSL spelling) during work on a new crematorium; informed Nallur Pradeshiya Sabha, then police and Jaffna Magistrate; investigation under CCPA No. 15 of 1979. "The first phase ... which involved a pit analysis, confirmed the existence of a mass grave." 2nd and 3rd phases followed. At publication: "over 200 human remains". OMP: "the seventeenth mass grave officially recorded in Sri Lanka".
- Met: Jaffna Magistrate Mr Amalavalan Anandarajah; JMO Dr Selliah Pranavan; CID officer in charge; Dr P.A. Dinesh Coonghe (Head, Forensic Medicine, Univ. of Jaffna); Prof. Raj Somadeva.
- p2-3 Main finding: institutions discharging duties with commitment, but "overarching gap in capacity and will among law enforcement authorities to ensure accountability". Main recommendation: new independent institution to investigate and prosecute serious crimes.
- p3: actors: Somadeva (PG Institute of Archaeology, Univ. of Kelaniya) + team; JMO Pranavan (forensic anthropology) + medical students; SOCO; Nallur PS workers; CID. OMP observer status. Remains stored at Dept of Forensic Medicine Univ. of Jaffna (secure, systematic); artefacts at Jaffna courthouse. Case No. BR/433/PC/25. Practice "relied largely on past experiences and a trial-and-error method".
- p4: Magistrate gave CID special permission for preliminary investigation; case conference at end of excavation. "over 90 percent of the human remains found at the site had no form of clothing whatsoever, thereby ruling out customary burials by Hindu adherents" (based on preliminary feedback of experts at site). Positioning, extreme proximity, shallow depth ("as shallow as 1.5-2 feet") -> "there is a reasonable likelihood that the burials were unlawful and pursuant to extrajudicial killings." Remains now stored in numbered paper bags then plastic boxes (vs polythene previously). GPR from Univ. of Sri Jayewardenepura. No formal SOP for mass grave investigations (draft exists).
- p6: 1999 Chemmani samples eventually sent to University of Glasgow. Recommend DNA bank of family samples. Lab equipment donated to Univ. of Jaffna for Clinical Genetics Unit held at Customs (VAT exemption pending since May 2025). Funding: JMO budget -> Magistrate -> MoJ via High Court; delays; many researchers and medical students work voluntarily.
- p7: "it is too early in the investigation process to draw any definitive conclusions on criminal responsibility." 2003 HRCSL Committee of Inquiry into Disappearances (Jaffna): 327 complaints received, 281 inquired into; CoI: arrests often before witnesses; "in nearly all cases they identified the institution as the army and, in a few cases, as the police". HRCSL: Army and Police "as institutions, are interested parties".
